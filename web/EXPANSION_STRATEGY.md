# TROVENDI expansion strategy

Status: **accepted for discovery, not committed for production delivery**.

This document records the architecture boundary for three future growth directions:

1. Cross-border CIS commerce.
2. Operating tools for manufacturers and omnichannel brands.
3. Wholesale network and a useful, moderated professional community.

The current execution priority remains the safe Wildberries vertical, production reconciliation, billing, AI Director and Integration Hub. Expansion work must reuse those foundations instead of creating a second financial or integration core.

## 1. Cross-border CIS

### Product boundary

TROVENDI may coordinate a seller, a local marketplace, logistics providers, fulfilment operators and a contracted local seller-of-record partner. TROVENDI is not itself described as a customs broker, tax adviser, payment institution or Merchant of Record unless the required contracts and legal permissions actually exist.

Country, tax, customs, marketplace-access and invoicing rules are versioned configuration backed by a named source and legal review. They are never generated by an LLM. Availability is shown per route and partner; the product must not make a universal claim that every Russian seller can or cannot trade directly on a marketplace.

### 1.1 Multi-currency Profit Center

Business logic:

- Store every monetary event in original currency and minor units; never overwrite it with a converted value.
- Attach transaction-time, settlement-time and reporting-time FX snapshots and identify the rate provider.
- Separate marketplace fee, local fulfilment, domestic leg, trunk leg, customs/brokerage, tax reserve, partner commission, payment fee and return/repatriation cost.
- Calculate unit contribution and cash result separately. Show FX gain/loss as its own line.
- Model non-redemption, damage, local disposal and reverse trunk delivery as explicit return scenarios, including weight and dimensions.
- A tax or duty result is `confirmed`, `scenario` or `unavailable`; missing legal inputs never become zero.

Required sources:

- Contracted marketplace sales, settlement, returns and fee feeds.
- Bank or approved FX-rate provider, with rate type and timestamp.
- Partner fulfilment, carrier, brokerage and seller-of-record statements.
- Versioned tax/customs rules reviewed for the legal entities and trade route in use.
- Product weight, dimensions, HS/EAEU classification and country of origin confirmed by the seller or documents.

Core tables:

- `currencies`, `fx_rate_snapshots`, `money_amounts`.
- `cross_border_routes`, `route_cost_versions`, `tax_rule_versions`.
- `customs_classifications`, `trade_documents`.
- `marketplace_settlement_lines`, `partner_settlement_lines`, `cross_border_cost_lines`.
- `return_scenarios`, `return_events`, `cross_border_profit_snapshots`.

### 1.2 Cross-cultural Card Factory

Business logic:

- Start from the immutable confirmed product fact set and create a locale-specific claim set.
- Generate native marketplace copy and search intent per locale; translation is only one pipeline stage.
- Validate required languages, fields, restricted claims, units and category dictionaries against a versioned marketplace profile.
- OCR text from each source image into addressable regions. Keep the untouched source and create a localized derivative with its locale, model, prompt, mask, font decision and checksum.
- Prefer editable text layers when a source design exists. Use inpainting only for raster images, then run OCR, overflow and visual-quality checks.
- Require human preview for every localized asset before first publication.

Required sources:

- Official or contracted marketplace category, attribute and content requirements.
- Seller brand glossary, prohibited translations and confirmed claims.
- Marketplace search/query data where a lawful supported feed is available.
- OCR, translation and image editing providers routed through the shared AI cost ledger.

Core tables:

- `marketplace_locale_profiles`, `brand_glossary_terms`, `localized_claim_sets`.
- `card_localizations`, `localization_validations`.
- `image_text_regions`, `localized_assets`, `asset_review_decisions`.
- Existing `ai_generations`, cost, publication and verification records remain authoritative.

### 1.3 Cross-border Smart Supply

Business logic:

- Forecast demand by country, marketplace, local warehouse and SKU.
- Represent lead time as a distribution: production, consolidation, domestic transport, border/customs, local transport and warehouse acceptance.
- Use conservative service levels and p90 arrival dates for stockout risk; do not plan from an average 10–25 day promise.
- Recommend quantity and destination from demand heat map, capacity, tariffs, minimum shipment, expiry/damage risk and working-capital limit.
- Recalculate when customs delay, acceptance capacity, sell-through or FX changes materially.

Required sources:

- Orders, returns and stock by local fulfilment node.
- Carrier milestones and observed route lead times.
- Customs/broker status where contractually available.
- Warehouse acceptance, storage, capacity and local delivery tariffs.
- Production capacity and purchase lead times when the manufacturer module is enabled.

Core tables:

- `fulfilment_nodes`, `route_legs`, `shipment_lots`, `shipment_milestones`.
- `lead_time_observations`, `lead_time_models`, `demand_forecasts`.
- `warehouse_demand_heatmaps`, `supply_plan_versions`, `supply_plan_lines`.

### 1.4 Partner network / seller of record

This is not the wholesale B2B Hub. It is a controlled operational network for routes where a contracted local entity sells and settles on the seller's behalf.

Business logic and API contract:

1. Create a route-specific partner agreement and capability profile.
2. Seller creates a shipment lot; partner acknowledges custody quantity and condition.
3. Partner maps seller SKU to local marketplace SKU and account without sharing unrelated tenants' data.
4. TROVENDI imports marketplace orders, returns, fees and payouts from an account or sub-ledger contractually scoped to the seller.
5. Partner submits signed settlement lines with source IDs, currency, tax document references and its fee.
6. Reconciliation matches custody, marketplace events, partner statement and bank settlement; differences enter a dispute queue.
7. Price/content/stock writes require capability checks, preview, seller approval, partner acceptance, idempotency key and post-write verification.

Core API resources:

- `/partner/v1/agreements`, `/catalog-mappings`, `/shipment-lots`, `/custody-events`.
- `/orders`, `/returns`, `/marketplace-fees`, `/settlements`, `/documents`.
- `/reconciliations`, `/disputes`, `/proposed-actions`, `/action-results`.

Core tables:

- `cross_border_partners`, `partner_agreements`, `partner_capabilities`, `partner_credentials`.
- `partner_store_scopes`, `partner_catalog_mappings`, `custody_ledger`.
- `partner_events`, `partner_settlements`, `partner_reconciliations`, `partner_disputes`.

### 1.5 Director risk management

Business logic:

- Deterministic engines calculate FX exposure, landed cost, margin, stockout probability and route concentration.
- Director explains evidence and proposes a bounded response; it does not invent a rate or legal rule.
- Price advice includes original/local currency, current and proposed margin, rate snapshot, fee version, expiry and sensitivity range.
- Stop-supply advice requires landed-cost and return-risk evidence. Automated price or supply changes remain blocked until their executor has preview, approval, limits and rollback/compensation behavior.

Core tables:

- `fx_exposures`, `route_risk_snapshots`, `margin_sensitivity_runs`.
- Existing Director recommendation, decision, execution, audit and measurement records are reused.

## 2. Manufacturer OS

### Production costing

- Versioned bills of materials and routings: raw materials, components, packaging, labour, subcontracting, scrap, machine time and allocated overhead.
- Purchase-price history and effective dates produce a traceable standard cost; actual production issues produce actual cost.
- Every marketplace profit snapshot points to the cost version used.

Core tables: `materials`, `bom_versions`, `bom_lines`, `routing_versions`, `operations`, `work_centers`, `cost_rates`, `production_batches`, `material_issues`, `finished_goods_receipts`.

### AI Production Planner

- Combines marketplace demand, safety stock, accepted supply plans, open orders, material availability and finite work-center capacity.
- Produces purchase proposals, production orders and due dates with constraint evidence.
- Human approval is required before purchase, production or supplier commitments.
- Integration Hub exchanges documents with 1C/MoySklad/ERP; TROVENDI must not become a second accounting source of truth by accident.

Core tables: `capacity_calendars`, `purchase_lead_times`, `material_requirements`, `production_plan_versions`, `production_orders`, `purchase_proposals`.

## 3. Omnichannel and wholesale

### Omnichannel inventory

- Use one canonical offer and inventory ledger across marketplaces, D2C, Avito, VK and future channels.
- Reserve stock transactionally before publishing availability; process channel events idempotently.
- Oversell protection, conflict policy and source freshness are mandatory before automated stock writes.
- Card Factory derives channel-specific copy from the same confirmed facts and brand rules.

### Wholesale Hub

- Customer groups, tiered price rules, MOQ, case packs, delivery terms and quote expiry.
- Generate traceable XLSX/PDF price lists and commercial proposals from confirmed catalog and price versions.
- CRM-style lead and quote workflow can precede full ordering. Payments, credit and legally binding documents are later regulated integrations, not an AI shortcut.

Core tables: `canonical_offers`, `channel_listings`, `inventory_ledger`, `inventory_reservations`, `channel_events`, `wholesale_accounts`, `price_list_versions`, `quote_requests`, `quotes`, `quote_lines`.

## 4. Professional community

The community launches only after there is enough active supply and demand. A blank forum is not an MVP.

First useful layer:

- Expert guides, product changelog and verified Q&A attached to TROVENDI modules.
- Private, verified manufacturer and distributor profiles with explicit visibility controls.
- Opt-in requests for distribution, fulfilment and production partnerships.
- AI thread summaries with source links and a visible `community content, not professional advice` label.
- Spam, manipulation, fake-review, buyout and prohibited-service detection with human appeal.

Data from Profit Center is never used for matching or benchmarking without granular opt-in. Aggregates require minimum cohort size and disclosure-resistant thresholds. Raw store economics never enter a public profile or model prompt.

Core tables: `community_profiles`, `verification_records`, `posts`, `moderation_events`, `partnership_requests`, `data_sharing_consents`, `benchmark_cohorts`.

## 5. Main technical and business risks

- Marketplace and partner APIs may be unavailable, incomplete or contract-limited.
- Legal, tax and customs rules differ by entity, route, product and effective date.
- Settlement timing and FX timing can make cash profit differ materially from order margin.
- SKU mapping and shared partner accounts create severe tenant-isolation and reconciliation risk.
- OCR/inpainting can change mandatory marks, dimensions or product appearance.
- Long and volatile lead times require observed distributions, not confident point forecasts.
- Inventory writes across channels can oversell when events are delayed or duplicated.
- Production planning becomes misleading when BOM, capacity or supplier lead-time data is stale.
- A community introduces cold-start, moderation, privacy, anti-competitive and fraud risks.

## 6. Sequenced validation roadmap

### Discovery gate

Before code, verify per marketplace and country: seller eligibility, official/partner API capabilities, data ownership, settlement documents, write permissions, tax/customs model, personal-data flow and at least two viable logistics/fulfilment partners.

### Candidate MVP: Kazakhstan read-only economics

Kaspi/Kazakhstan is the first candidate, not a committed launch promise. Proceed only if discovery confirms a contracted data route and legally workable partner model.

1. Import a partner-provided catalog, orders, returns, fees and settlement statement for 5–20 SKUs.
2. Build the multi-currency landed-cost and return-scenario ledger in read-only mode.
3. Reconcile one full settlement cycle and show evidence for every line.
4. Add Russian/Kazakh localization preview without publication.
5. Add one route lead-time model and warehouse recommendation.
6. Measure whether sellers accept the calculated margin and would pay for the service.

Uzum/Uzbekistan follows only after the same contract/API discovery, with bilingual content and non-EAEU customs handled as explicit additional complexity.

### Later gates

- Partner write actions only after reconciliation and tenant-isolation audits pass.
- Manufacturer costing after canonical products and accounting connectors exist.
- Production planning after trustworthy BOM, inventory and capacity data exist.
- Omnichannel stock writes after reservation and replay tests pass.
- Wholesale matching before a broad community; community feed after repeat active usage exists.
